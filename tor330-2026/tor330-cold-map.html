It is a cold-kit week, not a wet one.

A ridge sits over the western Alps for the whole race. Across all 79 checkpoints and all seven days the models produce no meaningful rain at all — the wettest single hour anywhere on the course is 0.6 mm, and peak rain probability never passes 19% while you are on the mountain. Snowfall is zero: the freezing level bottoms out near 3030 m, which is still above Col Loson, the highest point you touch.

So the decision at every life base is about cold and wind. Two things drive it. First, the ridge cools from Wednesday night onward — the freezing level falls from 4300 m to 3200 m and the high cols go sub-zero. Second, those nights are clear and almost windless, which is what makes them bite: nothing traps the heat, so exposed cols radiate hard and you feel several degrees below the air temperature the moment you stop moving.

How much to trust this

Forecast skill decays fast past four days, and this is a single model blend — cross-checks against a second source were only possible for the first two race days, where they agree closely.

The dry, ridge-dominated pattern is the robust part and is unlikely to break. The exact timing of Wednesday’s cool-down is the soft part — it could arrive twelve hours either side. Re-check at each life base.

If your race drifts

  • Every leg card’s second tile shows the felt temperature if you are running behind plan by the slack shown — up to twelve hours by the last leg.
  • Running late makes legs 6 and 7 colder, not warmer: you push deeper into the pre-dawn hours at altitude.
  • Wave 2 starters: add two hours to every time on this page.
  • A 100-hour race pulls Malatrà into Thursday evening and out of the coldest window entirely; a 130-hour race puts Vessonaz into Friday night instead.

The two cold decisions

  • At Valtournenche (km 237.5) take everything warm you own. Cuney to Vessonaz is two hours above 2650 m in the coldest part of Thursday morning, at or below freezing, in still air.
  • At Ollomont (km 286.5) keep it all. Col Malatrà at 2921 m around midnight is the coldest point on the entire course, and it has chains to hold in the dark.
  • Everything before Gressoney is a sun-and-water problem. Carry the mandatory shell, expect not to use it.
